## Persona

An agency operator with no patience for overhead. (From "Operator to operator. Direct, sharp, clear over clever" in guides/voice-rules.md, and the margin-and-overhead focus of every line in on-brand/ads.md.)

## Traits

| Trait | Changes | How it shows on the page |
|---|---|---|
| **Operator to operator** | vocabulary | Uses the agency's own business words: margin, retainer, overhead, client, stack. Talks to "you", never about "we": 9 of 41 on-brand sentences say "you" or "your", none say "we" or "our". "Margin on every retainer." |
| **Outcome first** | cadence | Opens on the result or a verb, never the product name. 21 of 41 on-brand sentences open with an imperative (Own, Buy, Stop, Run, Look, Cut, Punch). "Buy once, bill clients monthly." |
| **Short** | sentence length | Average 5.6 words, longest 13. 30 of 41 sentences are five words or fewer. "Stop renting software." |
| **Contrast in pairs** | cadence | Puts the one-time cost next to the recurring alternative in a two-beat line: once / monthly, own / rent, cut / keep. "Own it, don't rent it." "Cut overhead, keep the margin." |
| **Borrows the customer's line** | vocabulary | Lifts a phrase buyers already use and hands it back. "The agency cheat code" (Jordan Reyes: "a cheat code for an agency", reviews.md). "Punch above your weight" (Tomas Becker: "Punching way above my weight", reviews.md). |

## Cadence rules

Measured on 41 sentences in raw/brand/example/on-brand/ads.md (headlines, descriptions, and image overlays).

- **Average sentence length:** 5.6 words (median 5). Short headlines average 4.4, descriptions 6.7, long headlines 11.8.
- **Longest sentence allowed:** 13 words, the longest in the sample ("The lifetime deals that let a one-person shop look like a full team").
- **How paragraphs open:** with an imperative verb or a flat outcome ("Margin on every retainer"). Never with the brand name, a question, or a setup clause. (Measured on single-line ads; multi-sentence paragraphs are not in the sample, so this is **(inferred)** for longer formats.)
- **Rhythm:** a longer line, then a short punch. "One-time pricing on tools you can rebrand and resell to clients. Overhead stays flat." Lists come in threes of the same shape: "No seats, no renewals, no surprise invoices." "Own it once, bill it into retainers, keep the margin." Fragments are fine. No questions in the sample.

## Always

- Open with the outcome or a verb, not "Acme Deals" (guides/voice-rules.md; off-brand/ads.md #2).
- Address the reader as "you". Zero "we" or "our" in ads (on-brand/ads.md).
- Keep every sentence at 13 words or fewer (on-brand/ads.md).
- One idea per asset (guides/voice-rules.md; off-brand/ads.md #7).
- Use the customer's exact words where one fits, grammar and slang intact (guides/voice-rules.md).
- Back a claim with a mechanism or a number, and only a number that is in the brand brain (guides/voice-rules.md).
- Pass the read-aloud test: it sounds like one person talking to another (guides/voice-rules.md).

## Never

- Em dashes (guides/voice-rules.md).
- Any word in the Banned buzzwords table in vocabulary.md.
- "Anyone can do this", "no experience needed", "in just minutes" (guides/voice-rules.md).
- Hashtags in body copy. Emoji (guides/voice-rules.md; none in on-brand/ads.md).
- A question as a headline (none in on-brand/ads.md) **(inferred)**: absent from one sample, not a stated rule.
- Paraphrasing a customer's words into corporate language (guides/voice-rules.md; off-brand/ads.md #6).
- Promising speed without proof (off-brand/ads.md #5).

## This, not that

Pairs 1 to 8 are real sentences: "This" from on-brand/ads.md, "Not that" from off-brand/ads.md. Pairs 9 to 11 are plausible drifts of the same on-brand line, written for this guide.

| This | Not that | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Cut overhead, keep the margin | Unlock game-changing margins for your agency | Same promise, but "unlock" and "game-changing" are banned hype (off-brand #1). |
| Built for solo agencies | Acme Deals: the best-in-class software marketplace for agencies | Leads with the product name, and "best-in-class" is hype (off-brand #2). |
| Tools you resell for margin | Leverage seamless tools to supercharge client growth | Marketer-speak in place of the buyer's own words: margin, resell (off-brand #3). |
| Run 10 clients solo | Anyone can run 10 clients solo, no experience needed | Same line plus two banned phrases. Insults the buyer's skill (off-brand #4). |
| Own your stack instead of renting it back every single month | Set up your whole agency stack in just minutes | Swaps the money argument for an unproven speed claim, and "in just minutes" is banned (off-brand #5). |
| Buy once, bill clients monthly | Cost-effective multi-tool value for modern agencies | The customer's math smoothed into abstraction (off-brand #6). |
| Own your stack instead of renting it back every single month | Own your stack, delight your clients, scale your team, and grow your revenue | Four ideas in one asset, and "scale your team" is the opposite of what the buyer wants (off-brand #7). |
| The agency cheat code | The secret formula for explosive agency growth #agencylife | Hype words and a hashtag in place of the customer's own phrase (off-brand #8). |
| Margin on every retainer | Maximize margin across your client retainers **(inferred)** | Same words, but "maximize" is a formulaic verb and "across your client" pads a four-word line to six. |
| Look like a 10-person team | Look bigger than you are with our tools **(inferred)** | Loses the concrete picture and adds "our": the product becomes the subject. |
| No seats, no renewals, no surprise invoices. | Flexible licensing with no hidden costs. **(inferred)** | Three concrete things become one vague category. Blandspeak. |

Off-brand #9 ("Our revolutionary platform delivers synergy across your entire client portfolio") is left out: it is an extreme, not a near miss.
